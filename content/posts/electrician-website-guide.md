## Begin with the customer and the job

Explain whether you take domestic work, commercial work or both. Describe the actual services a customer can enquire about. A homeowner planning improvements and a business arranging maintenance may need different information.

Avoid turning the first screen into a list of abbreviations. Use plain headings, then add the detail needed to make an informed enquiry. The electrician should check the wording for technical accuracy before launch.

## Verify every credential

Registrations, qualifications, inspection services and regulatory terminology are not interchangeable. Use the precise current credential the business holds and confirm how it can be described or displayed. A badge should link to a relevant verification route where available.

Do not add a logo because it looks reassuring or assume that offering a service proves a particular accreditation. This article concerns website communication, not electrical safety or compliance advice; those details need the qualified business owner’s review.

## Give distinct services useful pages

If the business genuinely offers several different services, explain the scope, typical customer, information needed for a quote and next step for each. Do not promise a fixed price when a site assessment or further details are required.

Be explicit about any service limits. A clear account of what the business does is more useful than suggesting every type of electrical project can be handled.

## Put real work beside the explanation

Use permissioned photos of completed work, the team or the business. Add a short factual description where it helps. Avoid sharing customer addresses or sensitive premises details unnecessarily.

## Ask for enough to start the conversation

A first enquiry could include contact details, the property area, the service required and a short description. More detailed questions can follow once the electrician has assessed what information is needed.

Check that phone links work on a mobile and that forms reach the receiving inbox. If customers can message while you are on a job, explain that it is an enquiry route, not an emergency service or confirmed booking.

## Keep coverage and business details consistent

List the actual areas served and use the same contact details across the website and Google Business Profile. Do not create fictional branches to cover more towns. A genuine project from an area can support a useful local page when the customer has agreed it can be shown.
