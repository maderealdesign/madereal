## Separate repairs from planned projects

A visitor with an immediate roof problem wants to know whether you handle that work, cover their area and can be contacted. Someone considering a full replacement may want examples, an explanation of the process and what happens during a survey.

Use clear service names for the work you actually provide: repairs, replacement roofs, flat roofs or roofline services, for example. Do not list every possible roofing service simply to catch more search terms.

## Make availability accurate

If you offer emergency attendance, explain the real availability and coverage. Do not add “24/7” as a design feature when calls are not handled that way. A website should not encourage customers to expect a service the business cannot provide.

Keep technical advice within your actual expertise and have the roofing business approve it. A web designer should not invent diagnosis or safety instructions to make a page longer.

## Show projects with enough context

Use clear photos of actual work, with permission. A short caption can explain the type of job, the relevant service and the general location without exposing a customer’s private address.

Before-and-after photos can help, but they should show the same job honestly. Avoid presenting manufacturer photography as your installation. Explain your role if multiple contractors were involved.

## Be precise about trust claims

If the business mentions insurance, memberships or guarantees, confirm exactly what is current and what it covers. “Guaranteed work” without scope or conditions can create an expectation that was never intended.

Use real reviews with attribution. Do not place well-known directory or trade-body logos in a “trusted by” strip unless there is a genuine relationship and permission to present it that way.

## Make an initial quote request easy

Ask for the contact details, property area and a short description needed for the next conversation. Photos may help later. Do not make customers complete a technical survey they cannot answer just to contact you.

Keep a visible call route and a message alternative. Check both on mobile. If the enquiry is not an agreed quote or booked visit, explain what happens next rather than showing a misleading confirmation.

## Explain real coverage

A roofing business serving Burnley and Pendle can state those areas clearly. Separate local pages are useful only when they contain specific information or real projects, not when they are duplicated town lists.
