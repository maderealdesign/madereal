## First, check whether people are arriving

Do not assume every website has a conversion problem. If only a few relevant people visit, there may not be enough evidence to judge the offer. Look at the same date range in Search Console, website reporting and your actual enquiries.

Search impressions mean your result appeared. Search clicks mean someone clicked. Neither is a completed form, a qualified lead or a paying customer. Keep those steps separate.

## Follow the full journey

Use this sequence: **search exposure → website visit → successful enquiry → suitable job → customer**. Find the weakest measured step rather than optimising whichever part is easiest to redesign.

For example, many impressions with few clicks could mean the page ranks too low, appears for the wrong searches or has an unclear search title. Visits with no enquiries could mean weak intent, missing information, an unconvincing offer or a broken form. These are possibilities to test, not conclusions from one number.

## Check the message in the first screen

Can a stranger tell what you do and where you work? “Quality solutions for every project” says little. “Roof repairs and replacement roofs across Burnley and Pendle” is more specific, if it is accurate.

Then check the offer. If the homepage preview is free but the finished service costs £35/month, say both clearly. Surprise pricing later can attract the wrong enquiry and undermine confidence.

## Run five practical tests

1. **Open the page on a phone.** Can you read the main message without zooming? Does anything cover the button?
2. **Try the menu and every contact route.** Confirm call and email links point to the correct details.
3. **Submit a clearly labelled test enquiry.** Check it reaches the receiving system and inbox. A thank-you animation alone is not delivery proof.
4. **Look for missing answers.** Services, coverage, process, expected costs and proof should be easy to find.
5. **Ask someone unfamiliar with the business.** Give them a task, such as requesting a roof-repair quote, and watch where they hesitate.

Fix a failed test before adding more advertising or publishing ten new articles.

## Make trust specific

Use real work, genuine customer feedback and accurate qualifications. A screenshot can show design quality; it cannot prove that a website generated a certain number of jobs. If you have outcome data, explain the period, source and limitations.

## Reduce the first commitment

A form that asks for a full brief, budget and every account login may be too much for a first conversation. Ask for what you need to respond, then gather the rest. Explain what happens next and avoid promising an instant response you cannot deliver.

Change one meaningful thing, record the date and compare a sensible period with enough relevant visits. Do not call a single extra enquiry a proven uplift.
