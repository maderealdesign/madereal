# Can I See My Website Design Before I Pay?

Yes. A website preview lets you see the direction of your new business website before you commit to the finished build.

That is useful because a website is difficult to judge from a sales call or a list of features. You need to see whether the design feels right for your business, whether the wording is clear and whether a customer would know what to do next.

## What should a website preview show?

A useful preview should show more than a colour palette or a generic template.

It should help you judge:

- how your business will be introduced;
- what customers will see first on mobile;
- how your services will be explained;
- where calls and enquiries will come from;
- how your reviews, photographs and local area can be presented;
- whether the overall design feels trustworthy.

The preview is not expected to contain every final page, photograph and detail. Its job is to make the direction clear enough for you to make an informed decision.

## What information does the designer need?

You do not need to write an enormous brief.

The most useful starting information is:

- your business name;
- the services you provide;
- the towns or areas you cover;
- your preferred contact details;
- a Google Business Profile, Facebook page or existing website if you have one;
- any photographs, reviews or branding you already use.

For local businesses, public information from a Google Business Profile can often provide enough context for the first draft.

## What should you check before approving it?

Look at the preview like a customer, not like a designer.

Ask:

1. Can I understand what this business does within a few seconds?
2. Does the page feel credible?
3. Is the main phone or enquiry action obvious?
4. Does it work clearly on a mobile screen?
5. Does the writing sound like the real business?
6. Are the services and locations accurate?

Small design details can be adjusted. The most important question is whether the structure makes the business easier to choose.
