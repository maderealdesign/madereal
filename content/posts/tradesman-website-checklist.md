## Decide which jobs you want

Do not start with “we do everything”. List the services you actually want enquiries about, then explain them in words customers use. A joiner fitting kitchens and doors needs a different first message from a builder taking on extensions.

Make the difference between planned and urgent work clear. Do not advertise round-the-clock availability unless someone really provides it. A service promise on the website becomes a customer expectation.

## Use honest proof, even when you are new

You may not have a large gallery yet. Use the genuine work you can show, your own business photos and a clear description of the process. If a photograph shows work completed while employed elsewhere, get permission and explain your involvement accurately.

Do not invent reviews or imply stock photography is a finished job. Describe qualifications, registrations and insurance accurately and keep the evidence current. A simple honest page is stronger than a wall of borrowed trust badges.

## Build a useful first set of pages

A homepage should introduce the business and main services. Distinct services deserve their own information where the questions differ. Add a work gallery or project pages, a clear coverage section and a short contact or quote route.

For each service, explain the type of work, who it is for, what information helps you quote and what happens after an enquiry. Avoid publishing multiple pages that repeat the same paragraph with a different heading.

## A Burnley and Pendle launch example

Imagine a newly independent joiner covering Burnley, Colne and Nelson. This is an **illustrative planning example**, not a claim about a client’s results.

Their first website could lead with fitted kitchens and internal joinery, show permissioned job photos and explain the actual travel area. The quote form might ask for contact details, location and a short description, then request measurements and photos in the follow-up conversation. A longer page about work in a particular town would only be useful if there is genuinely local information to add.

That is a clearer starting point than a generic “Lancashire construction solutions” page with no examples and a long application form.

## Test the quote route

Open it on a phone. Tap the number. Try the WhatsApp link. Submit a labelled test form and verify that it arrives. Make sure a fixed button does not cover the last field or an error message.

Explain that you will respond about the job; avoid promising a quote before you have enough information to assess it. If you cannot answer calls while working, give customers another way to leave details.

## Give search engines useful information

Use a descriptive title, real service coverage, clear headings and links between related pages. Keep your Google Business Profile details consistent with the website. These are foundations, not a guarantee of a certain number of jobs.
